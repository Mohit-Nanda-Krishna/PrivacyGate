4. Definitions
The following definitions apply throughout this policy and all subordinate risk documentation.
TERM DEFINITION
Risk The effect of uncertainty on the Group's objectives, expressed as a combination of the consequence of an event and the
likelihood of its occurrence.
Inherent risk The level of risk before consideration of any mitigating control, assuming controls are absent or wholly ineffective.
Residual risk The level of risk remaining after operation of controls assessed as designed and operating effectively.
Target risk The level of residual risk the Group intends to achieve following completion of agreed treatment actions.
Risk appetite The aggregate level and type of risk the Group is willing to assume in pursuit of its strategic objectives, approved by the
Board.
Risk tolerance The permissible variation around an appetite measure before escalation is triggered. Tolerance is narrower than the limit.
Risk limit A hard quantitative boundary that must not be breached. A limit breach is a policy breach.
Risk capacity The maximum risk the Group could absorb given capital, liquidity, operational capability and regulatory constraints.
Risk owner The named individual accountable for management of a specific risk, including adequacy of its controls and treatment.
Recorded in the register by name and employee identifier — for example, RSK-4488 is owned by Naomi K. Osadebe
(EMP-41077).
Control owner The named individual accountable for design, operation and testing of a specific control.
Control A measure that modifies risk, being preventive, detective or corrective, and either manual or automated.
Key control A control on which primary reliance is placed to keep a risk within appetite. Subject to mandatory independent testing.
Issue An identified weakness in the design or operation of a control, or a gap between the current and required state.
Incident An event that has occurred and caused, or had potential to cause, loss, disruption, regulatory breach, customer
detriment or reputational damage.
Near miss An event with potential to result in loss or detriment that did not do so, whether through control operation or through
chance.
Loss event An incident resulting in quantifiable financial loss, whether or not the loss has yet been booked.
Key risk indicator (KRI) A quantifiable measure used to monitor the level of, or change in, a risk exposure over time.
Key control indicator (KCI) A quantifiable measure of the operating performance of a control.
RCSA Risk and Control Self-Assessment: the structured process by which the first line identifies risks, assesses controls and
rates residual exposure.
Risk acceptance A documented, time-bound decision to tolerate a residual risk without further treatment, taken by an authorised approver.
Exception (waiver) A documented, time-bound approval to operate outside a requirement of this policy or a subordinate standard.
Emerging risk A risk newly developing or changing, whose impact or likelihood is not yet well understood and cannot yet be reliably
quantified.
Important business service A service delivered to an external end user, the disruption of which could cause intolerable harm to customers or market
integrity.
Impact tolerance The maximum tolerable duration or extent of disruption to an important business service.
Material risk taker An individual whose professional activities have a material impact on the Group's risk profile, as defined by the
Remuneration Committee and recorded by name, employee identifier and role in the MRT Register.
Personal data Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person, including name, employee identifier, corporate or
personal e-mail, telephone number, home address, date of birth, national identifier and photographic image.
Three lines model The accountability structure separating risk ownership (first line), oversight and challenge (second line) and independent
assurance (third line).
